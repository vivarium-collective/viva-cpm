Q Layered on the validated Increment-2 virus field + infection transition: does a diffusing type-I IFN field, converted per-cell into a resistance scalar (source formula `resist = f_bar/(a_rf+f_bar)`), gate an infected cell's virus secretion by `(1-resist)` strongly enough to measurably REDUCE infection spread and total virus versus the same run with no IFN (Increment 2's path, unchanged)?

Claim

A type-I IFN field, sampled locally per infected cell into a resistance scalar (source formula) that gates that cell's virus secretion by `(1-resist)`, measurably reduces total virus versus an identical no-IFN run (same seed, same 60-update driver) — roughly HALVED at every sampled step (819.16 vs 1750.69 at step 20, ~53%; 1222.88 vs 2752.34 at step 30, ~56%; 1624.58 vs 3905.38 at step 40, ~58%). This is the study's PRIMARY, robust quantitative evidence. Infected-cell count (n_I) moves in the same protective direction but is a WEAKER signal at the step Task 3.3's integration test actually asserts: 48 vs 50 at step 20 is only a ~4% reduction; the gap widens to ~15% at step 30 (51 vs 60) and ~23% at step 40 (53 vs 69) as the virus-load reduction has more time to compound into a visible count difference. So n_I is treated here as a corroborating directional trend, not co-equal quantitative evidence with total_virus. This is a MECHANISM-validation claim (the IFN->resistance->reduced- secretion chain behaves in the protective direction and has a real, non-trivial magnitude on virus load, with a smaller/slower-to-emerge effect on cell counts), not a claim that any of Sego et al. 2022's quantitative figure targets (Figs 3B/5/7) are met — that reproduction verdict remains PENDING until Increment 9 (the capstone).

Infected epithelial cells secrete type-I interferon (IFN) into a second diffusing, decaying extracellular field (source-cited diffusion coefficient, decay rate, and secretion constant, per `pbg_cpm_studies/influenza/params.yaml`'s `ifn:` block), layered on the Increment-2 virus field. Each update, every currently-infected cell reads its own locally-diffused mean IFN concentration and converts it into a resistance scalar via the CC3D-source formula `resist = f_bar/(a_rf+f_bar)` (params.yaml `resistance:` block; NOT the paper's printed `1 - f_bar'/(theta*a_f+f_bar')` form — every source consumer applies `(1-resist_source)` wherever the paper's rho appears protectively, matched here). That resistance throttles the SAME cell's own virus secretion for the next update by a factor of `(1-resist)`, so a cell that has accumulated more local IFN releases proportionally less virus. Measured against an identical no-IFN run (Increment 2's path, unchanged), this produces a substantial, sustained brake on VIRUS LOAD — total_virus is the study's primary, robust quantitative evidence, roughly halved already at step 20 (~53%) and staying in that ~53-58%-reduction regime through step 40. Infected-cell COUNT (n_I) moves in the same protective direction but only weakly at step 20 (~4%, 48 vs 50 cells); the reduction widens to a clearly meaningful ~23% by step 40 (53 vs 69) as the virus-load reduction has more time to compound into fewer new infections — a corroborating trend, not evidence as strong as total_virus's. mean_resist itself reaches a fast local steady state (~0.53-0.57) almost immediately rather than climbing toward saturation — because the IFN field's fast decay and diffusion drive each infected cell's local IFN to a quasi-steady concentration within about one update, and that concentration happens to sit close to (about 1.2x) the resistance formula's `a_rf` constant rather than far above it. There is no cell death/recovery, no Allee effect, no IL-10 cross-talk, no immune cells (macrophages/NK/CD8+ T), and no APC-driven global IFN amplification feedback yet — all later increments.

Test

Wire the type-I IFN field (Task 3.2, `pbg_cpm_studies/influenza/fields.py` `add_ifn_field`) and the pure per-cell resistance function (Task 3.3, `pbg_cpm_studies/influenza/resistance.py::cell_resistance`) into a new driver, `run.run_virus_infection_with_ifn` (Task 3.3), alongside the unmodified Increment-2 `run_virus_infection`, and compare their per-update series (n_I, n_H, total_virus, plus `mean_resist` recorded only by the with-IFN driver) at identical seed/parameters (Task 3.3's integration test `tests/test_influenza_virus_infection.py::test_ifn_resistance_slows_spread_vs_no_ifn` and the fuller with/without series in Task 3.3's report). A minimal in-package figure (`pbg_cpm_studies/influenza/viz.py::ifn_resistance_figure`, Task 3.4) renders both series side by side plus the mean_resist trajectory.

Result

A per-cell resistance scalar computed purely from each infected cell's OWN locally-sampled IFN (no explicit cell-cell signaling beyond field diffusion) is sufficient to cut total virus roughly in half from the earliest sampled step onward — that virus-load reduction is the robust, well-supported effect. Infected-cell count responds to the same mechanism but more slowly: the n_I gap is small at step 20 (~4%) and only widens to a clearly meaningful ~23% by step 40, because fewer infections have had time to compound into a visible count difference even though virus release is already substantially throttled. The protective effect is real, but it comes from resistance hitting a fast local steady state (~0.55) almost immediately, not from resistance climbing toward saturation (~1) as more IFN mass nominally accumulates in the field over time.

Conclusion

MECHANISM VALIDATED, NOT REPRODUCED. The type-I IFN field drives a per-cell resistance scalar that gates virus secretion and measurably reduces total virus relative to the Increment-2 no-IFN path (same seed): total_virus is the PRIMARY, robust quantitative evidence, roughly halved at every sampled step (~53% at step 20, the step Task 3.3's integration test actually asserts, through ~58% at step 40). n_I moves in the same protective direction but is a WEAKER, corroborating signal at the tested step (48 vs 50, ~4% at step 20), widening to a more meaningful ~23% by step 40 (53 vs 69) as the effect compounds — n_I alone at step 20 is not strong evidence of slowed spread. mean_resist confirms the gate engages (>0 from step 1 onward) but plateaus at ~0.55 rather than ramping toward 1 — a flagged Increment-9 calibration/convention question, not resolved here. This increment has no death/recovery, no Allee effect, no IL-10 cross-talk, no immune cells, and no APC-driven global IFN amplification, and makes NO claim about matching any of Sego et al. 2022's Figs 3B/5/7 quantitative acceptance bands — that reproduction verdict is PENDING until Increment 9 (the capstone).

Plan & assumptions

Key assumptions

  • APC-driven IFN amplification is entirely omitted — in this study, IFN comes only from directly-infected cells' own local secretion, not from the global ODE-compartment feedback loop the paper models (Increment 8). The measured protective effect here is therefore a LOWER bound on what a full model with amplification might show.
  • Resistance in this study ONLY gates virus secretion (via `set_cell_secretion_scale`). The source also uses resistance to modulate infected-cell death probability and the model's Allee-effect term, and IL-10 secretion cross-talks with it — none of that exists yet (later increments).
  • OPEN CALIBRATION QUESTION (flagged for Increment 9, not resolved here) -- mean_resist plateaus at ~0.55 (f_bar ≈ 1.2x a_rf), not climbing toward 1, because each infected cell's local IFN reaches a fast secretion/diffusion/decay steady state that happens to land close in magnitude to `a_rf` (8.5e-6) — a plausible but "suspiciously just right" coincidence between two independently-derived constants (Task 3.3's report). Worth cross-checking against the source's actual field magnitudes/resist trajectories before Increment 9 locks in a reproduction verdict.
  • Single seed (17) for both drivers (same two-RNG-stream convention as Increment 2) — the with/without comparison is a single-run demonstration of the mechanism's qualitative and approximate quantitative behavior, not a statistically characterized effect size across a systematic replicate sweep.
  • As in Increment 2, there is no live-demo process-bigraph composite for the WITH-IFN driver — `run_virus_infection_with_ifn` is a raw `cpm_core.World` loop (Task 3.3), not wrapped as a composite. The `conditions.baseline` entry below reuses the SAME Increment-2 live-demo composite (`pbg_cpm_studies.composites.influenza.viral_infection`, no IFN/resistance wiring) purely so this study has a dashboard-loadable baseline; it is NOT the composite this study's readouts are measured from, and it does not reproduce the IFN mechanism. All readouts above come directly from `run.run_virus_infection_with_ifn` / `run.run_virus_infection`.
  • The with-IFN driver `run_virus_infection_with_ifn` is ~4-5x slower per step than `run_virus_infection` (two diffusing fields, IFN needing 40 vs virus's 5 stiff-diffusion substeps per MCS) — a performance note for Increment 9's longer/larger reproduction runs, not addressed here.
