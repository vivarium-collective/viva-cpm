Q Layered on the validated Increment-1 confluent sheet: does a diffusing, decaying extracellular virus field (source-cited D/decay, target diffusion length 5 cell diameters = 25 sites) and a stochastic, virus-driven H -> I infection transition produce LOCAL lesion spread (new infections cluster near existing infected cells, not uniformly across the sheet) with population conserved?

Claim

The extracellular virus field diffuses and decays as specified, and a seeded lesion of infected cells spreads via a stochastic, virus-driven healthy-to-infected transition that stays local to existing infection, with total cell population conserved throughout. This validates the mechanism only — it is not a claim that any of Sego et al. 2022's quantitative figure targets are met; that reproduction verdict remains pending until the capstone increment.

A small seeded lesion of InfectedReleasing (I) epithelial cells secretes virus into a diffusing, decaying extracellular field (source-cited diffusion coefficient and decay rate; target diffusion length 5 cell diameters = 25 lattice sites, per `viva_cpm_studies/influenza/params.yaml` `virus:` block). Nearby Healthy (H) cells stochastically transition to Infected as a function of the local mean field concentration they experience (`Pr(infect) = 1 - exp(-g_hv * v_bar)`), so the lesion grows outward from its seed rather than infecting uniformly at random across the sheet. This reproduces the qualitative spatial-spread PHENOMENON that underlies Sego et al. 2022's Fig 6 virus/infection spatial snapshots (referenced qualitatively here — Fig 6 has no digitized acceptance band in this workspace, unlike Figs 3B/5/7, which are cell-count time-series targets, not spatial-pattern targets, and are therefore not the right comparison for this study). There is no resistance/IFN response yet (per-cell resistance stays 0 throughout — Increment 3 adds the type-I IFN field and the resistance state variable that will begin to gate this same transition), no cell death/recovery, and no immune cells.

Test

Wire the virus field (Task 2.1, `viva_cpm_studies/influenza/fields.py`) and the stochastic infection transition (Task 2.2, `viva_cpm_studies/influenza/transitions.py`) together over the Increment-1 sheet, drive them for 60 updates from a small seeded lesion (`viva_cpm_studies/influenza/run.py::run_virus_infection`, Task 2.3), and report the measured spread/locality/null-control behavior from the integration test (`tests/test_influenza_virus_infection.py`). Wrap the same mechanism as a process-bigraph composite (`viva_cpm_studies.composites.influenza.viral_infection`, CPMProcess + InfectionProcess wired through `fates`) for the dashboard live demo (Task 2.4, this study) — measurements come from `run_virus_infection`, not the live-demo composite (see caveat and Task 2.3's report on why the two paths use independent RNG streams and are not bit-identical).

Result

A population-conserving, spatially-local infection spread emerges from just two mechanisms (field diffusion + a per-cell stochastic H -> I draw driven by local field concentration) with no explicit "infect-my-neighbor" rule — new infections land ~0.6x as far from prior infection as a uniform-random null baseline (a discriminating test that a shuffled-field non-local control correctly fails at ~1.13x), because the field itself is spatially concentrated, not because the transition rule encodes locality directly.

Conclusion

MECHANISM VALIDATED, NOT REPRODUCED. The virus field diffuses/decays and drives a population-conserving, spatially-local infection spread from a seeded lesion (measured: n_I 9 -> 16, 7 new-infection events landing ~0.61x as far from a prior-infected cell as a uniform-random null baseline would predict — a discriminating test a shuffled-field non-local control correctly fails at ratio ~1.13; null control confirms no spontaneous infection). This increment has no resistance/IFN gating, no death/recovery, and no immune cells, and makes NO claim about matching any of Sego et al. 2022's Figs 3B/5/7 quantitative acceptance bands — that reproduction verdict is PENDING until Increment 9 (the capstone).

Plan & assumptions

Key assumptions

  • Virus secretion magnitude uses the documented z=1/per-pixel convention (`viva_cpm_studies/influenza/fields.py`'s `_virus_field_params` — the source's per-cell `secretion_g_vi` is halved to remove the source's z=2 slab thickness for our single-layer z=1 sheet, then spread evenly over each cell's 25 pixels). This reproduces the source's RATE convention faithfully but its exact absolute magnitude against the paper's published field scale is an Increment-9 calibration concern, not validated here.
  • resist=0 for every cell throughout this run — no IFN-driven resistance gating exists yet (Increment 3). The measured infection rate here is therefore an UPPER bound on what later increments will show once resistance suppresses some fraction of would-be infections.
  • Single seed (17) for both the initial-lesion choice and the stochastic transition (two independent streams, see `run.run_virus_infection` docstring) — the 7-event, ~0.61x-null-ratio locality result is a single-run demonstration (the ~0.58-0.67x range cited above spans a small number of seeds/step counts checked during test development, not a full replicate study), not a statistically characterized rate across a systematic replicate sweep.
  • The live-demo composite (`viva_cpm_studies.composites.influenza.viral_infection`) and the measurement driver (`run.run_virus_infection`) use independent RNG streams for the same `seed` and are NOT expected to produce bit- identical trajectories (Task 2.3 report) — the composite exists for a fast dashboard demo, not as the source of this study's readouts.
