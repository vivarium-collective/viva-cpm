Q With every mechanism subsystem from Increments 0-8 wired into ONE per-MCS loop (`run.run_full_model`, Task 9.1), does sweeping that model over Sego-2022 Fig-5's five initial-viral-load scenarios (1x, 10x, 100x, 1000x, 10000x the ODE-calibrated dose, uniform virus-field IC, zero pre-infected cells) reproduce the paper's central Fig-5 finding -- a MONOTONE, threshold-like dose-response in which the spatial model stays non-lethal for multipliers up to 1000x and trends toward lethal (near-total uninfected-cell loss) only near 10000x -- and, honestly, does a REDUCED-scale sweep (few loads/replicas, a short population and run duration, the only scale a fast CI test can afford) show the monotone DIRECTION even though it cannot match the digitized bands' absolute magnitudes at paper scale?

Claim

`run.repro_fig5` is implemented and wired correctly: it sweeps a seeded ensemble of `run_full_model` over Fig-5's viral-load scenarios, maps every observable onto `targets/fig5.json`'s exact keys, ensemble-means per load via `bands.aggregate_replicas`, filters each load's own scenario-tagged band subset (`_fig5_target_subset`, verified to raise `ValueError` rather than silently mixing scenarios when a load has no tagged entries), and evaluates via `_evaluate_fig5_subset`, producing a well-formed `{"by_load": {load: {...}}, "lethal_threshold": ..., "band_eval": {...}, "loads":..., "replicas":..., "cells_per_side":..., "steps":...}` dict (verified by `tests/test_influenza_full_model.py:: test_repro_fig5_viral_load_sweep`). `run.repro_fig5` sweeps the Task-9.1 capstone driver (`run_full_model`, every Increment 0-8 mechanism in one source-ordered per-MCS loop) over Sego-2022 Fig-5's initial-viral-load scenarios (a uniform virus-field IC at 1x/10x/100x/1000x/10000x the ODE-calibrated dose, zero pre-infected cells, at 10000 epithelial cells / 50 replicas at paper scale) and compares each load's ensemble mean against that load's own digitized acceptance-band subset (`targets/fig5.json`, filtered per load by the scenario-grouping guard `_fig5_target_subset` -- the guard the Task-9.0 review flagged as load-bearing, since the raw file interleaves all 5 loads' bands in one flat list per observable). UNITS FIX (Task-9.3-review MUST-FIX, this update, F-04): a prior version of this study attributed part of that gap to a "virus-field concentration-units mismatch"; that was actually a structural raw-sum-vs-mean BUG in `repro_fig5`'s observable mapping (`run_full_model`'s raw per-lattice-site sum was fed straight to extracellular_virus instead of the per-site concentration `targets/fig5.json` digitizes). The qualitative mechanism this task's reduced-scale test IS built to check -- a MONOTONE, more-virus-worse-outcome dose-response -- holds cleanly: seeding a higher initial viral load (10000x) leaves the ensemble with ZERO surviving uninfected cells (0/144) by this short run's end, vs 88/144 (61%) surviving at the lowest tested load (1x). `lethal_threshold` (the smallest tested load crossing 50% uninfected loss) comes out as 10000, the same load Sego-2022's own Sec. 3.3 identifies as where the spatial model trends toward lethal outcomes -- a DIRECTIONAL echo of the paper's finding, not a magnitude or threshold-VALUE reproduction (only 2 of the paper's 5 loads were tested here, so this reduced run cannot establish whether THIS model's own threshold sits between 1x and 10000x the way the paper's does between 1000x and 10000x). THIS STUDY DOES NOT CLAIM FIG-5 IS REPRODUCED: the paper-scale 50-replica, all-5-load, 10000-cell, ~15-day ensemble (`repro_fig5`'s own docstring documents this as `replicas=50, cells_per_side=35, steps≈3086`) is the Mac-mini Phase-B follow-up that will actually set the `reproduced` verdict for Fig 5. No band in `targets/fig5.json` was edited and no model constant was tuned toward a better-looking number.

Result

load=10000's uninfected_cells/antibodies land FULLY in-band (6/6 each, worst_miss 0) while load=1's do not (4/6 and 5/6) -- but this is a scale/duration coincidence, not evidence load=10000 is individually better-calibrated: at load=10000 the model's uninfected count collapses to 0 within the first record (near-total, near-instant infection at this reduced 144-cell scale and uniform-IC seeding), and fig5.json's own load=10000 bands are the WIDEST and most permissive of any load's (e.g. t_days=3: lo=50,hi=3000; t_days=15: lo=1,hi=300) precisely because the paper itself reports the highest replica-to-replica spread at the lethal-threshold dose (Sec. 3.3) -- a flat-extrapolated near-zero value happens to sit inside that wide band at every checkpoint. load=1's bands are comparatively tighter and its reduced-run infected fraction (56/144 ~ 39% infected by t~0.068 days) is already far above the target's load=1 trajectory (infected_cells stays in the 1-8 range even at t_days=15). The honest read: the DIRECTION of the dose-response (more initial virus -> more, faster infection -> fewer surviving uninfected cells) is correctly reproduced by the wired mechanism; the MAGNITUDE/timing match to fig5's digitized bands is not, driven by population-scale (144 vs 10000 cells) and run-duration (~0.068 vs up to 15 simulated days) mismatch at this reduced config, not by an incorrect mechanism. UNITS FIX (Task-9.3-review MUST-FIX, this update, supersedes the prior "extracellular-virus units mismatch" reading below): the ~1200x extracellular_virus miss this study previously reported at BOTH loads was a STRUCTURAL BUG, not a real calibration/PFU-scale gap -- `repro_fig5` was mapping `run_full_model`'s RAW per-lattice-site SUM of `field_conc` straight to `extracellular_virus`, while `targets/ fig5.json` digitizes that observable as a PER-SITE CONCENTRATION (its t=0 value for a given `viral_load_multiplier` IS that multiplier itself). Reporting the field's SPATIAL-MEAN concentration instead (`sum(field_conc)/n_lattice_sites`, `n_lattice_sites` from this run's own `params.dims`) resolves the sites-factor: extracellular_virus's worst_miss drops from ~1200 to 0.208 (load=1, still 4/6 in-band, not fully) and to 0.0 (load=10000, now FULLY IN-BAND, 6/6). A SMALL, HONEST RESIDUAL remains and is NOT tuned away: at t=0 the spatial-mean model value is ~0.375x the target (e.g. load=1 -> mean 0.375 vs target 1.0; load=10000 -> mean 3750 vs target 10000) -- both same order of magnitude as the target, not thousands of times off. This ~0.375 residual factor is the ratio of the seeded PATCH's site count to the FULL simulation domain's site count (the domain reserves margin/ separation sites around the patch for the immune-cell clusters and recruit reserve pool, per `run_full_model`'s `margin_sites`/ `separation_sites`; the virus is seeded only under the patch, so averaging over the larger full-domain lattice dilutes the concentration by the patch/domain area ratio). This is a real, distinct, honestly flagged domain-geometry effect, not a units bug -- do not read it as evidence the units fix is incomplete or tune `margin_sites`/ `separation_sites` to close it.

Plan & assumptions

Key assumptions

  • REDUCED-SCALE REPORT, NOT PAPER-SCALE REPRODUCTION -- every band_eval number in this study.yaml comes from the exact `loads=(1,10000), replicas=1, cells_per_side=12, steps=15, seed0=0` config this task's fast pytest test uses, run directly to produce these numbers honestly, NOT from any paper-scale (50-replica, all-5-load, 10000-cell) run. The Mac-mini Phase-B follow-up (documented in `repro_fig5`'s own docstring) is the run that will set any `reproduced` verdict.
  • ONLY 2 OF 5 LOADS SWEPT AT REDUCED SCALE -- `loads=(1,10000)`, chosen to keep the reduced test's fast dose-response assertion meaningful (the largest available load-ratio contrast) without paying for a 5-load sweep in the fast test suite; loads 10/100/1000 are exercised only by `repro_fig5`'s default signature (`loads=(1,10,100,1000, 10000)`), not by this study's reported numbers, and are NOT swept at paper scale until the Mac-mini Phase-B follow-up.
  • RUN-DURATION MISMATCH -- the reduced config's 15 records x `mcs_per_step=7` x `s_per_mcs=60` = 6300s = ~0.068 simulated days falls far short of `targets/fig5.json`'s checkpoint window (0.0-15.0 days); `bands.series_in_band`'s flat extrapolation carries the model's near-t=0 (or, for load=10000, near-instant-collapse) value out to every later checkpoint, which mechanically drives most of the reported misses -- a documented property of this reduced config, not a bug in `bands.series_in_band` (Task 9.0's, unmodified here).
  • POPULATION-SCALE MISMATCH -- `cells_per_side=12` gives 144 epithelial cells vs. `targets/fig5.json`'s `scenario.total_epithelial_cells=10000` (`cells_per_side=35`, `patch_mm=1.0` at paper scale); raw population counts (uninfected/infected cells) cannot match the target's absolute magnitudes at this reduced population even with perfectly calibrated rate constants.
